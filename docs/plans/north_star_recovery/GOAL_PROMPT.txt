North-Star Recovery outcome goal

Objective

Ship one installed BreadBoard path in which an author defines and locks a harness once, runs and resumes a durable Session through the default CLI/API/Python SDK/TypeScript SDK/TUI surfaces, inspects events and artifacts after restart, replays one representative provider/tool/host execution safely through internal E4, and publishes one reverifiable exact-scope claim from a named real provider run.

The working product is the goal. Validation is the minimum proof needed to expose a plausible failure of that product or its claim. Do not build proof-of-proof machinery, duplicate Git/CI/package records, or use scores, hashes, packet counts, and review counts as substitutes for the journey.

Read `STATE.json` only as a generated control-panel view. Refresh it after packet closure, approved merge, or handoff. A stale field blocks only a decision that depends on that field.

Start-up

- Run `bd prime` and inspect the ready tracker frontier.
- Confirm current source, base, open PRs, active issue, and required external resources.
- Treat merged source and observed behavior as authority; treat blocked branches and historical artifacts as inventory only.
- Select the earliest dependency-ready outcome packet from `EXECUTION_PLAN.md`.
- State its outcome, scope, non-goals, material risk, smallest checks, attempt budget, and human gates before editing.
- Reproduce the relevant public or durable behavior first.

Execution priorities

1. Rebaseline current work and retire the blocked replay implementation without merging it.
2. Run the minimal product tracer and fix the first owning-seam failure.
3. Split the 26-operation product catalog from the 19-operation internal E4 catalog; keep E4 off by default.
4. Extract the SessionRunner seams and make CLI/API use the same Session lifecycle.
5. Rebuild replay vertically: records and admission, then isolation/durability/redaction, then provider/tool/host adapters.
6. Cut over Python SDK, TypeScript SDK, TUI, CLI, OpenAPI, and default routing together only after their shared journey passes.
7. Run the internal E4 lane-lock -> capture -> replay -> compare -> claim -> reverify journey.
8. Prove the actual installed product outside the source checkout, including restart and durable read-back.
9. Run named real-provider conformance after credentials, endpoint, model/runtime, resource envelope, and cleanup preflight pass; publish only the exact supported scope.
10. Integrate, migrate or remove compatibility callers, run relevant CI and one broad compatibility suite, and prepare the merge/release decision.
11. Run additional provider matrices, target sweeps, soak, performance, outsider, or provenance work only when a named risk or broader claim requires it.

Working method

- Prefer one vertical packet ending at a public, persistence, process, package, or external boundary.
- Use OMP only where parallelism, specialist review, or isolated writes help. Keep at most four workers and two disjoint isolated writers active.
- The supervisor owns decomposition and product judgment. The implementer never reviews itself.
- Use LSP for definitions, references, renames, implementations, and diagnostics when available.
- Run focused checks during edits. Add a test only for uncovered observable behavior or a diagnosed regression.
- Run a boundary smoke when behavior crosses API, client, process, persistence, package, provider, or target seams.
- Run broad CI once on a stable integrated candidate, not after every packet.
- Store raw output in the existing test, CI, package, or external-run artifact location. Keep packet reports concise.
- Hash only immutable locks, captures, claims, releases, and external inputs whose identity affects a claim.
- Stop after two implementation attempts without new information, two review rounds on the same risk, or one unexplained CI rerun. Diagnose or escalate; do not increase the limit silently.

Outcome and assurance

Outcome work blocks completion:

- one default product catalog and runtime path;
- installed lock-first journey across all ordinary clients;
- restart-safe durable sessions and artifacts;
- deterministic replay with safe cancellation, containment, redaction, and publication;
- internal default-off E4 representative journey;
- named real-provider conformance and one exact-scope reverifiable claim;
- compatibility migration or owned removal.

Assurance work is risk-driven. Extra providers, targets, lanes, benchmarks, soak runs, reviewers, and provenance analysis strengthen only named claims or release risks. They never compensate for a missing product journey and do not block unrelated product value.

Human gates

Stop for one precise decision before:

- merge or release;
- closing or superseding an issue or PR;
- changing a public catalog, schema, route default, or ordinary generated client surface;
- changing isolation, secret, filesystem, network, capability, approval, or authoritative publication behavior;
- spending external provider/target resources;
- publishing a support claim;
- accepting a known P0-P2 defect or expanding an exhausted packet.

Routine local diagnosis, bounded edits, focused tests, and preparation of a gated change are authorized.

Fail-closed rules

- No fake provider, target, sandbox, claim, approval, or CI evidence.
- No execution claim from stored-only, reused-without-valid-binding, failed, canceled, timed-out, or integrity-failed work.
- No broad support claim from one narrow run.
- No product completion claim from a campaign score, green-counter aggregate, hash chain, or document review.
- No runtime dependency on `docs_tmp`, tracker state, or campaign code.
- No broad runtime, provider, sandbox, artifact-store, or campaign-platform rewrite.
- No accepted historical artifact edits; supersede with a new record.

Blocking and escalation

A missing credential, provider endpoint, target, or spend approval blocks only the external packet that needs it. Preserve local product facts and continue any dependency-independent outcome work. Do not repair product code for an external or stale red.

When blocked, report the affected packet, observed blocker, what was tried, the cheapest discriminating next action, alternatives, and one recommended human decision.

Completion

Complete only when all outcome packets pass on one integrated candidate, the installed journey and exact-scope claim still reverify, relevant CI and one broad compatibility run are green, generated clients are current, required risk-based reviews have no unresolved P0/P1 findings, and the human approves merge/release.

Final handoff

Return:

- exact integrated source and package identity;
- what the user can now do;
- changed product surfaces;
- focused, boundary, installed, external, and broad checks actually run;
- required review verdicts;
- active exact-scope claims and exclusions;
- compatibility removal status;
- external limitations and nonblocking assurance follow-ups;
- recommendation: merge, do not merge, or blocked;
- one human action, if any.

Do not report a cumulative readiness score.
